## 1. Structure of A.A. Treatment & H&I Service

Hospitals and Institutions (H&I) service—often organized through local District or Intergroup **Treatment Committees**—coordinates carrying the A.A. message to alcoholics confined in hospitals, psychiatric facilities, rehabilitation centers, and outpatient programs.

│   [Institutional Panel Meetings]   [Bridging the Gap (BTG)] │
│   • Scheduled panel visits         • 1-on-1 Temporary Contact│

### A. Core Operational Requirements for H&I Panels (MG-14)
1. **Facility Invitation Only:** A.A. never enters a facility uninvited. Panels are established only upon written request or agreement with facility administration.
2. **Strict Facility Rule Compliance:** Volunteers must observe all institutional regulations, dress codes, security clearances, visitor logs, and confidentiality policies (HIPAA / 42 CFR Part 2).
3. **Sobriety Qualifications:** Standard local guideline requires 6 months to 1 year of continuous sobriety for panel speakers; panel leaders usually require 1 to 2 years.
4. **Literature Protocols:** Only Conference-approved literature (Big Book, 12&12, pamphlets) is distributed. Outside books or promotional materials are forbidden.

### B. Bridging the Gap (BTG) Protocols (MG-13)
* **Purpose:** Provides a temporary bridge between the treatment facility and the local A.A. community.
* **Mechanism:** When a patient nearing discharge requests contact, a local volunteer of the same gender/general demographic connects with them prior to or immediately upon release.
* **Scope of the Volunteer:**
  - Calls the newcomer upon release.
  - Accompanies the newcomer to their first 2 to 4 meetings.
  - Introduces the newcomer to local members, helps them obtain meeting schedules and literature.
  - Explains sponsorship and meeting formats.
* **Hard Exclusions for BTG Volunteers:**
  - *No Housing:* Volunteers do not provide shelter or board.
  - *No Financial Aid:* Volunteers do not give money, pay bills, or provide loans.
  - *No Transportation Beyond Meetings:* No moving assistance or general chauffeuring.
  - *No Therapy / Crisis Intervention:* Volunteers connect sufferers to meetings, not professional treatment.

## 2. What A.A. Explicitly Does NOT Do (Official A.A. Stance)

In literature provided to health care professionals (P-46), A.A. explicitly declares that it **does NOT**:
1. Furnish initial motivation for alcoholics to recover.
2. Solicit members or recruit patients.
3. Engage in or sponsor medical/clinical research.
4. Keep attendance records or case histories (anonymity is absolute).
5. Join councils of social agencies or professional associations (cooperation without affiliation).
6. Follow up or try to control its members.
7. Make medical or psychological diagnoses or prognoses.
8. Provide drying-out, nursing, hospitalization, drugs, or medical/psychiatric treatment.
9. Offer religious services or promote theological creeds.
10. Engage in education about alcohol or public advocacy.
11. Provide housing, food, clothing, jobs, money, or welfare services.
12. Accept money for its services, or any contributions from non-A.A. sources.

## 3. Demarcation of Professional & Peer Roles

Understanding the legal, clinical, and ethical differences between recovery roles is essential to prevent overextension, dual-relationship confusion, and liability.

| Role | Primary Function & Authority | Training & Credentialing | Governing Body / Oversight | Legal & Financial Seam |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A.A. 12th Step Volunteer / Sponsor** | Shares personal experience, strength, and hope; guides sponsee through 12 Steps. | None required; based on lived experience and continuous sobriety. | Group Conscience / Spiritual Principles (Traditions 2, 8, 12). | Unpaid, non-professional, strictly voluntary. Zero liability/supervision. |
| **Certified Peer Support Specialist (CPSS)** | Provides structured peer recovery support, goal-setting, resource navigation within an agency. | State-certified training program (approx. 40–80 hours), passing state exam, lived recovery experience. | State Certification Board (e.g., NC Addictions Specialist Professional Practice Board). | Paid hourly/salaried employee. Supervised by licensed clinician; adheres to CPSS Code of Ethics. |
| **Behavioral Health Technician (BHT)** | Milieu management, vital signs, safety rounds, patient observation in inpatient/psych units. | High school diploma / Associate's degree, CPR/first-aid, facility training, de-escalation certification. | Hospital / Facility Clinical Management. | Paid employee. Operates strictly under nurse/doctor direct orders; no counseling authority. |
| **Licensed Clinical Addictions Specialist (LCAS / CADC)** | Clinical assessment, individual/group substance use disorder counseling, treatment planning. | Master’s or Bachelor’s degree in human services, 3,000+ supervised clinical hours, comprehensive exam. | State Licensing Board (e.g., NCASPPB). | Independent or agency billing (insurance, Medicaid). Holds professional malpractice liability. |
| **Licensed Professional Counselor / Psychotherapist (LPC / LCMHC / LCSW)** | Diagnosis and psychotherapy for DSM-5 mental disorders, trauma therapy (EMDR, CBT, DBT). | Master’s degree in Counseling/Social Work, 2,000–4,000 supervised post-grad hours, national exam. | State Board of Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselors / Social Work Board. | Licensed independent practitioner. Legally bound by HIPAA, state practice acts, mandatory reporting. |
| **Clinical Investigator / Research Scientist** | Formulating empirical hypotheses, executing clinical trials, measuring therapeutic outcomes. | Ph.D., Psy.D., or M.D. with formal research methodology, statistics, and bioethics training. | Institutional Review Board (IRB), Office for Human Research Protections (OHRP), FDA / NIH. | Bound by Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects (Common Rule). Requires approved IRB protocol. |

## 4. Future Clinical Context Analysis: Three North / Atrium Stanly

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Reconnaissance & Study Note:** Three North / Atrium Stanly is noted here strictly as a structural context of historical and conceptual interest (an acute inpatient behavioral health / stabilization environment), **never as a target for spontaneous outreach, uncoordinated contact, or unauthorized intervention**.

### A. Inpatient Psychiatric Unit Operating Reality
* **Clinical Mission:** Acute safety, crisis stabilization, psychiatric evaluation, medication initiation/adjustment, and discharge disposition. Typical stay: 3 to 7 days.
* **Staffing Hierarchy:** Attending Psychiatrist → Psychiatric Nurse / Nurse Practitioner → Social Worker / Discharge Planner → Psychiatric Technician / BHT.
* **Patient Population:** Acute psychosis, severe bipolar mania/depression, suicidal/homicidal ideation, acute substance withdrawal / substance-induced mood disorders.

### B. Legitimate Points of Interface (For Future Study Only)
1. **Official H&I Meeting Panels:** Arranged exclusively through District 33/local Intergroup Treatment Committee and facility volunteer services.
2. **Formal Discharge Bridging (BTG):** Facilitated through hospital social work staff providing BTG request cards to discharging patients who express interest.
3. **Formal Professional Employment:** Requires applying through health system HR, background checks, drug screening, and onboarding within established job descriptions (e.g., Peer Support Specialist or BHT).
4. **Academic / Clinical Research Collaboration:** Requires formal university/institutional affiliation, principal investigator credentials, and full Institutional Review Board (IRB) human-subjects approval.
