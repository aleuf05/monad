## 1. *A.A. for Alcoholics with Mental Health Issues — and their sponsors* (Pamphlet P-87)

* **Publication Authority:** General Service Conference-approved (A.A.W.S., revised 2018).
* **Target Audience:** Dual-diagnosis members, sponsors, mental health professionals.
* **Core Themes & Operational Principles:**
  1. *De-stigmatization of Psychiatric Illness:* Explicitly affirms that mental illness (clinical depression, bipolar disorder, PTSD, schizophrenia, severe anxiety) is an illness just like diabetes or heart disease.
  2. *Dual-Track Treatment:* Step work and psychiatric treatment are complementary. Working the 12 Steps does not replace psychiatric care; psychiatric medication does not replace the spiritual program.
  3. *Sponsor Guidelines for Mental Health:*
     - Sponsors are spiritual mentors, not psychologists or psychiatrists.
     - Sponsors should encourage sponsees to seek professional help when symptoms of mental illness appear.
     - If a sponsee expresses suicidal thoughts, the sponsor must immediately guide them to professional emergency services or crisis hotlines (e.g., 988), not rely solely on prayer or a meeting.

## 2. *A.A. as a Resource for the Health Care Professional* (Pamphlet P-46)

* **Publication Authority:** General Service Conference-approved (A.A.W.S.).
* **Target Audience:** Medical doctors, psychiatrists, clinical psychologists, nurses, hospital social workers.
* **Core Themes & Operational Principles:**
  1. *Demystifying A.A. for Medicine:* Outlines the fellowship's structure, 12-Step mechanics, and meeting types (Open vs. Closed).
  2. *Referral Mechanics:* Explains how clinicians can introduce patients to A.A. via local Intergroup/Central Office answering services and hospital meeting lists.
  3. *The Anonymity Guarantee:* Assures healthcare professionals that patient privacy is protected by A.A.’s bedrock tradition of personal anonymity.

## 3. *Living Sober* (Book B-5)

* **Publication Authority:** General Service Conference-approved (A.A.W.S., 1975).
* **Target Audience:** Newcomers in early sobriety; practical daily living.
* **Core Behavioral Techniques (Day-to-Day Stability):**
  1. *Physical Stabilization:*
     - Eating or drinking something sweet (glucose replacement for physiological alcohol cravings).
     - Proper sleep, nutrition, and avoiding physical exhaustion (HALT framework).
  2. *Cognitive & Habit Interruption:*
     - "Never Drink Alone" & "Don't Take the First Drink."
     - Changing physical routines, avoiding bars, walking different routes home.
     - "Twenty-Four-Hour Plan": Breaking sobriety down into manageable 24-hour, 1-hour, or 10-minute increments.
  3. *Emotional Slogans as Operating Macros:*
     - *Easy Does It:* Dampens frantic overachievement and urgency.
     - *First Things First:* Prioritizes sobriety above all competing demands.
     - *Live and Let Live:* Dissolves resentment and frustration with difficult personalities.

## 4. *A.A. Guidelines: Treatment Committees* (MG-14 / M-40i)

* **Authority:** General Service Office of A.A. (G.S.O.).
* **Key Operating Rules:**
  - Panels must always be formed by invitation of the facility.
  - A.A. members do not comment on patient medication, clinical diagnoses, or length of inpatient stay.
  - Panel coordinators ensure rotating speakers so patients hear diverse stories of recovery.
  - Financial separation: Committees purchase literature through group 7th Tradition funds; no facility money is accepted.

---

## 5. *A.A. Guidelines: Bridging the Gap* (MG-13)

* **Authority:** General Service Office of A.A. (G.S.O.).
* **Key Operating Rules:**
  - One-on-one temporary contact matching: male with male, female with female.
  - Initial contact made before or immediately upon facility discharge.
  - Volunteer accompanies newcomer to 2–4 outside local meetings.
  - Boundaries: No housing, no cash loans, no therapy, no general rides.

## 6. *A.A. Guidelines for Members Employed in the Alcoholism Field* (MG-05)

* **Authority:** General Service Office of A.A. (G.S.O.).
* **Key Operating Rules:**
  - Dual-Hat Separation: A member working as a counselor or technician must strictly separate their professional job from their A.A. membership.
  - Prohibition of Client Solicitation: A member must never use A.A. meetings to solicit clients for a paid private practice or treatment center.
  - Anonymity Protection: Professional staff who are A.A. members maintain public-level anonymity at press/media levels.

## 7. *The A.A. Service Manual & Twelve Concepts for World Service*

* **Authority:** General Service Conference of A.A. / Bill W.
* **Key Structural Governance Concepts:**
  - *Concept III (Right of Decision):* Trusted servants and service boards are given the operational freedom and authority to exercise their best judgment without micromanagement.
  - *Concept IV (Right of Participation):* Voting and participation rights are extended to all active workers in service structures, preventing caste systems.
  - *Concept IX (Leadership):* True leadership in A.A. is based on dedication, vision, tolerance, and personal example, never executive power or command authority.
  - *Concept XII (General Warranties of the Conference):* The spiritual charter ensuring that the Conference never becomes dangerous, wealthy, or authoritarian; maintains non-violence and open financial transparency.
