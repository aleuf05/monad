# Safety

Monad's safety model protects human authority while allowing useful thought to
be preserved. Its central distinction is between **internal reasoning** and
**external action**.

## Consistent operating principles

1. **Human strategic authority.** Cameron sets direction and retains final
   authority over consequential action, canon, publication, deletion, and
   material changes to agent authority.
2. **Bounded AI roles.** AI officers act only within named responsibilities,
   current instructions, and standing delegation. Advice and capability do not
   create authority.
3. **Approval before external action.** Publication, communication, spending,
   deployment, account or permission changes, hardware control, destructive
   operations, and comparable effects require the applicable human gate.
4. **Least privilege.** Approved work uses only the access and authority needed
   for the defined result. Delegation for one action is not a general grant.
5. **Private/public separation.** The private Root Console may hold memory and
   command surfaces. Public experiments receive only deliberate projections
   and do not inherit private context or Root Console authority. A public demo
   may have documented control over its own simulated state without crossing
   this boundary.
6. **Preserve without executing.** Monad may capture a thought, draft, question,
   or proposed action without performing, publishing, or promoting it.
7. **Reasoning is not action.** Internal analysis and simulation remain
   distinguishable from externally observable changes. Crossing that boundary
   requires an explicit, inspectable authorization path.

These principles summarize existing records; they do not create a new
operational policy or override a more specific adopted doctrine.

## Thought-to-action boundary

```text
thought or observation
        |
        v
preserved draft / internal reasoning
        |
        v
human review and applicable approval gate
        |
        v
least-privilege execution
        |
        v
external effect + evidence + knowledge capture
```

A captured thought can stop at any earlier stage. Preservation is successful
even when no action follows.
