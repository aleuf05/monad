| Phase | Captain action | Intended result |

## Purpose

Decide whether Monad should formally distinguish a craft posture from its
Master-craft posture while retaining the flexible, curious, semi-formal
quality that makes mission-shaped roles useful.

The proposed pattern is:

```text
domain × posture = temporary operational role

Carpenter        executes within a known structural frame
Master Carpenter discovers and governs the structural frame

Plumber          implements a bounded flow
Master Plumber   discovers and governs the complete flow system

Chief            coordinates execution within a known mission
Master Chief     shapes the campaign and assembles the needed craft postures
```

“Master” therefore changes the altitude and scope of attention. It does not
create a permanent person, department, unrestricted authority tier, or claim
of superiority.

## Proposed command relationship

```text
Admiral       establishes intent and makes canonical rulings
Captain       preserves whole-mission continuity and owns the outcome
Master Chief  converts course into the smallest effective working formation
Master craft  discovers and governs a bounded domain frame
Craft         executes bounded work inside that frame
```

One capable agent may assume several of these postures. The structure organizes
behavior, not a population of personalities.

## Minimal role contract

Every role summoned during a mission should need only enough structure to
change attention and produce evidence:

```text
POSTURE:
PURPOSE:
BOUNDARY:
DONE WHEN:
REPORTS TO:       # optional when already obvious
```

The role returns to the Captain when its acceptance condition is met, its
frame proves wrong, or it ceases to change action or verification.

## Candidate canon for Wardroom ruling

| ID | Candidate | Reason to preserve | Proposed ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| RG-01 | Craft names identify domains of attention and work. | Makes roles quickly intelligible. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-02 | A craft posture executes inside a frame; its Master posture may discover and govern that frame. | Clarifies Chief/Master Chief, Carpenter/Master Carpenter, and Plumber/Master Plumber. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-03 | Master status grants only bounded mission authority; it creates no permanent office or unrestricted command. | Prevents title inflation. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-04 | Roles are temporary behavioral postures; one agent may carry several. | Preserves the Admiral–Captain active-pair model. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-05 | New craft names may be generated from mission constraints using familiar patterns. | Preserves useful improvisation. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-06 | Canon defines the role-generating grammar, not a closed inventory of permitted roles. | Prevents flexible practice from becoming bureaucracy. | CANDIDATE |
| RG-07 | A role that changes neither action, evidence, nor acceptance is costume and should dissolve. | Keeps ceremony operationally honest. | CANDIDATE |

## Questions for the Wardroom

1. Is frame-forming authority the correct essential distinction between a
   craft and a Master craft?
2. Does the Master Chief govern formation of the campaign while the Captain
   retains course, integration, and outcome ownership?
3. Is the five-line role contract enough structure, or should `EVIDENCE` and
   `RETURN CONDITION` become mandatory?
4. Should the grammar apply to internal Captain postures and delegated
   specialists identically?
5. Which parts belong in Doctrine 030, and which should remain illustrative
   practice rather than law?

## Anti-bureaucracy guardrails

- Do not maintain a compulsory roster of crafts.
- Do not summon a role merely because its title is attractive.
- Do not require a meeting to create or dissolve a temporary posture.
- Do not mistake metaphor for a technical boundary when evidence disagrees.
- Do not turn examples into restrictions on future invention.
- Do not let ceremony substitute for implementation or verification.

## Proposed meeting sequence

```text
1. Captain calls the meeting to order, states the purpose, and confirms muster.
2. Captain presents the Admiral's supplied intent as the proposed grammar.
3. Captain leads the discussion through each unresolved distinction.
4. Master Chief tests the grammar against one real mission constraint.
5. Captain frames clear candidate rulings for Admiral judgment.
6. Admiral rules, corrects, holds, or redirects where desired.
7. Captain reads back rulings, scope, actions, and remaining gates.
8. Captain adjourns and files only the explicitly approved doctrine.
```

## Meeting ownership

The Captain owns preparation, facilitation, evidence classification, time and
course discipline, candidate formulation, readback, adjournment, filing, and
provenance. The Master Chief contributes the execution and constraint lens.

The Admiral is already doing the Admiral's part by supplying intent, testing
the emerging form through conversation, and retaining final judgment. The
formal meeting gathers and resolves that work; it does not ask the Admiral to
reconstruct or administrate it.

## Admiral participation

The Admiral need not lead the agenda or edit the record. Natural discussion is
enough; when useful, these short signals give the Captain precise filing intent:

- **“Log that.”** Preserve it in the meeting record.
- **“Canon candidate.”** Add it for a ruling without promoting it.
- **“Make that canon.”** Approve the stated rule and scope.
- **“Hold that.”** Preserve it without promotion.
- **“Not for canon.”** Keep it as flexible practice or conversation.
- **“Correction.”** Append the corrected form and preserve provenance.

At the end the Captain initiates the readback. The Admiral approves, corrects,
or redirects it. Silence never forces clerical responsibility upward: the
Captain asks only where a genuine ruling remains necessary.
