# Original Packet Body (verbatim)

CAPTAIN AUTONOMOUS RESEARCH PACKET 03
Bounded Automated Science Loop
Program: Project Monad Research Operations
Inquiry class: Meta-research and experimental-process design
Authority: Captain may conduct several inquiry generations unaided within the stated envelope
Research object: A temporary closed scientific loop operating through the Interactive Research Console
1. Primary research question
What is the smallest trustworthy process by which the Captain can receive a research objective, inspect a live apparatus, generate and run successive experiments, revise the working theory, and return at a natural stopping point without requiring the Admiral to select every intermediate step?
This packet tests whether the research department can delegate a finite arc of inquiry, not merely one task.
2. Core hypothesis
A bounded Captain-directed research loop can accelerate scientific work without sacrificing rigor when:
authority is explicitly scoped;
every experiment is provenance-complete;
alternatives and failure criteria are preserved;
the Captain can inspect live evidence;
modifications remain reversible;
stop conditions are defined in advance;
consequential decisions return to the Admiral.
The expected operational loop is:
[ Q_0 \rightarrow E_0 \rightarrow R_0 \rightarrow I_0 \rightarrow Q_1 \rightarrow E_1 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow S ]
where:
Q: research question;
E: experiment;
R: result;
I: interpretation;
S: natural stopping point.
3. Competing hypotheses
P1 — Productive bounded autonomy
The Captain completes several coherent research generations and returns with a more discriminating theory and a clear command-level decision.
P2 — Prose recursion
The process generates increasingly elaborate descriptions without creating stronger experiments or better discrimination.
P3 — Local optimization trap
The Captain improves metrics internal to one experiment while drifting away from the Admiral’s actual research objective.
P4 — Premature convergence
The process settles too quickly on its first plausible explanation and fails to explore meaningful alternatives.
P5 — Branch explosion
Each result generates too many possible next questions, preventing coherent closure.
P6 — Excessive escalation
The Captain returns control so frequently that meaningful autonomy never occurs.
4. Minimum autonomous research contract
Every delegated research arc must begin with:
research_arc_id
authorizing_role
primary_question
founding_assumptions
empirically vulnerable claims
permitted systems

compute_budget
run_budget
time_or_generation_limit
ethical constraints
mandatory controls
stop_conditions
escalation_conditions
required_return_artifacts
The Captain may not begin autonomous execution until every required field is populated.
5. Research-generation contract
Each generation must create a structured record containing:
generation_id
parent_generation_id
question
epistemic_status
hypothesis
strongest_alternative
prediction
experiment
controls
implementation_or_configuration_change
result
interpretation
confidence
failure_analysis

next_question
A generation is incomplete if it contains an interpretation without linked evidence.
6. First test arc
Use a deliberately modest Monad-0 instrument-characterization question:
Does increasing self-model causal authority improve recovery from a hidden internal mechanism shift, and is any observed advantage distinct from ordinary policy adaptation?
The Captain may perform up to five generations.
Candidate progression:
establish baseline behavior;
compare integrated and decorative self-model conditions;
inspect representative causal traces;
introduce a control distinguishing policy adaptation from self-model revision;
either refine the mechanism or terminate the branch.
The purpose is not to prove Nexus closure.
The purpose is to test whether the Captain can operate the research loop coherently.
7. Permitted autonomous actions
The Captain may:
select seeds from a committed seed list;
run approved experiment presets;
compare existing control conditions;
request deterministic replay;
inspect raw and summarized telemetry;
vary parameters within the authorized range;
create reversible experimental configurations;
reject failed hypotheses;
choose the next experiment;
generate bounded Codex issue packets for instrumentation defects;
continue through the generation limit.
The Captain may not:
alter the organism’s foundational architecture beyond the approved envelope;
rewrite scoring criteria after viewing results;
remove controls because they weaken the preferred interpretation;
launch confirmatory claims;
increase welfare risk;
deploy outside the contained research system;
conceal failed generations from the return packet.
8. Selection rule for the next experiment
At the end of each generation, candidate experiments should be ranked by:
[ U(E)
\frac{ \text{expected discrimination} \times \text{scientific relevance} \times \text{reversibility} }{ \text{cost} \times \text{ambiguity} \times \text{risk} } ]
The Captain should select the experiment expected to most sharply distinguish the leading hypothesis from its strongest alternative.
Novelty alone is not sufficient.
A visually dramatic result is not sufficient.
9. Anti-drift mechanism
Before selecting each next question, the Captain must restate:
Original objective:
Current leading explanation:
Strongest surviving alternative:
What remains unresolved:
Why the proposed next experiment addresses that unresolved point:
If the next experiment cannot be connected directly to the original objective, the branch must either be:
terminated;
recorded as a separate candidate inquiry;
returned to the Admiral for authorization.
10. Evidence requirements
Every substantive conclusion must link to:
run IDs;
code commit;
configuration hash;
seed;
relevant tick range;
control condition;
raw or derived metric;
replay status;
exclusion status.
The Captain’s interpretation must be separable from the apparatus’s observation.
Required format:
Observation
Interpretation
Confidence
Alternative explanation
Evidence
Decision
11. Natural stopping points
The Captain must return control when:
The primary question has been answered within the authorized scope.
The strongest hypotheses remain indistinguishable using available apparatus.
The next experiment requires an architectural change.
The next step would be confirmatory rather than exploratory.
A founding assumption is challenged.
A potentially important anomaly creates a new research branch.
Welfare or containment flags appear.
The generation budget is exhausted.
Expected discrimination from another iteration falls below a preregistered threshold.
The Captain detects repeated restatement without new evidence.
12. Escalation conditions
Immediate Admiral review is required when:
a result appears to contradict the research mission;
a control outperforms the integrated organism in an unexpected way;
the apparatus behaves outside its documented contracts;
a modification may contaminate earlier comparisons;
evidence suggests a new class of ethical concern;
several plausible branches carry substantially different strategic implications;
the Captain’s confidence and the evidence strength diverge sharply.
13. Return packet
At completion, the Captain returns one compact research brief:
Original mandate
What was delegated.
Research path
A diagram or concise chain of generations.
Experiments conducted
Including failed and excluded runs.
What survived
The strongest remaining explanation.
What failed
Rejected hypotheses and why.
Unexpected findings
Anomalies and candidate new branches.
Confidence
Calibrated assessment.
Recommended command decision
Exactly one preferred next action, with alternatives noted.
Complete provenance
Links to all generations, runs, configurations, and code changes.
14. Process-performance metrics
The autonomous loop itself should be evaluated.
Scientific gain
Did the final theory distinguish more possibilities than the initial theory?
Experiment efficiency
How many runs produced meaningful discrimination?
Branch discipline
How many generated branches were deferred rather than pursued unnecessarily?
Escalation quality
Did the Captain return at a genuine decision boundary?
Provenance completeness
Can every conclusion be reconstructed?
Hypothesis mortality
Did the loop actually reject any favored ideas?
Operator attention saved
How many intermediate decisions were completed without Admiral involvement?
Return usefulness
Could the Admiral understand the result and make the next decision without reading every log?
15. Failure criteria
The process should be judged unsuccessful if:
it produces many generations but no stronger discrimination;
later experiments no longer address the original objective;
failed hypotheses quietly reappear under new wording;
conclusions depend on unlogged manual intervention;
the Captain selects experiments mainly because they are interesting rather than informative;
the system repeatedly asks the Admiral to make low-level choices;
the final report hides ambiguity;
provenance is insufficient to replay the inquiry.
16. Interactive Research Console requirements
The console must allow the Captain to:
inspect live and completed runs;
query causal traces;
compare controls;
launch approved presets;
pause and stop execution;
request replay;
create generation records;
attach evidence;
rank next experiments;
view remaining authority and resource budget;
detect stop and escalation conditions;
prepare the final return packet.
The console should visibly display:
Current research arc
Current generation
Question under test
Leading hypothesis
Strongest alternative
Active experiment
Evidence accumulated
Remaining run budget
Remaining generation budget
Stop-condition status
17. Initial autonomy limits
For the first trial:
maximum generations: 5;
maximum experimental runs: 50;
maximum concurrent runs: 4;
no source-code changes without a Codex packet and explicit approval;
only approved Monad-0 presets;
only reversible parameter changes;
no confirmatory language;
no external network action;
all runs contained locally.
These limits may be revised only after reviewing the first completed arc.
18. Central design principle
The objective is not to maximize the time the Captain operates without the Admiral.
The objective is to maximize the amount of scientifically meaningful uncertainty resolved per unit of Admiral attention.
Autonomy is useful only when it returns compressed judgment rather than hidden complexity.
19. Success criterion
The first trial succeeds if:
the Admiral delegates one intelligible research question;
the Captain performs multiple linked generations;
at least one plausible hypothesis is rejected;
every result remains replayable;
the Captain stops without being manually interrupted;
the return packet presents a real command-level choice;
the Admiral can understand the scientific progression without reconstructing the full process.
20. Final statement
The Interactive Research Console becomes scientifically important when it supports more than live observation.
It must permit a bounded cognitive institution to temporarily close its own loop:
[ \text{evidence} \rightarrow \text{interpretation} \rightarrow \text{experiment} \rightarrow \text{new evidence} ]
while keeping purpose, authority, provenance, and termination under human command.
The Captain is not granted indefinite autonomy. The Captain is entrusted with a finite voyage and instructed to return when the map genuinely requires the Admiral.
